Security Deposit Interest Calculator

Some states and cities make landlords pay interest on a tenant's security deposit, and each one has its own rate and rules. Pick where the rental is, enter the deposit and the dates, and this works out the interest owed for each year of the tenancy, using the official rate for each year and the law's own rules on who's covered, part years, and when it's due. For landlords working out a payment and tenants checking one. Everything runs in your browser.

San Francisco security deposit interest rates

The Rent Board sets each rate from the prior year's average 90-day AA financial commercial paper rate. Each one runs from March 1 to the end of February. The rate that applies to a year's interest is the one in effect on the annual due date, not the one in effect when the deposit was paid.

Example (the Rent Board's own): a $2,000 deposit paid November 1, 2024. On November 1, 2025 the rate in effect is 5.0%, so $100 is due. On November 1, 2026 it's 4.2%, so $84 is due: $184 for the two years. If the tenant instead moved out May 1, 2026, the second year would be prorated: $2,000 × 4.2% × 181 ÷ 365 = $41.65, due within two weeks. Landlords of rent-controlled units may deduct half of the year's Rent Board fee from the interest.

Quick examples for the fixed-rate states

  • Massachusetts: a $1,500 deposit earns 5%, or $75.00, each tenancy year (less if the bank paid a lower rate).
  • Ohio: a $1,500 deposit with $1,000 monthly rent: only the $500 over one month's rent earns interest, so 5% × $500 = $25.00 a year.
  • Minnesota: a $1,200 deposit paid January 15, 2024 and returned in March 2026 earns interest for February 2024 through March 2026, 26 months: $1,200 × 1% × 26 ÷ 12 = $26.00.
  • New York: a $3,000 deposit in an account paying 3.5% in a 20-unit building: the landlord keeps 1%, so the tenant gets 2.5%, or $75.00 a year.

How the calculator works

It splits the tenancy into years starting from the date the deposit was paid. A full year earns the deposit times that year's rate, as simple interest. A part year at the end is prorated by days, except in Maryland and Connecticut, where the calculator counts full months and gives each month 1/12 of the rate for the calendar year the month starts in. Minnesota counts months the way its statute does. Where a law uses the bank's rate (New York, a lower Massachusetts rate, an Illinois lease from before 2026), type the rate in. The rates are built in through February 2027 for San Francisco and through 2026 for Connecticut and Maryland; a year without a published rate shows as "not published yet".

Things the calculator doesn't do: Connecticut's rule that drops a month's interest when the rent was more than 10 days late without a late fee, the San Francisco Rent Board fee deduction, any deduction the landlord is allowed for damages, and local rules other than San Francisco's. Some cities have their own deposit interest ordinances, so check local law too.

FAQ

What is the security deposit interest rate for 2026?

San Francisco: 4.2% (March 1, 2026 to February 28, 2027). Connecticut: 0.49%. Maryland: 3.47%. Massachusetts and Ohio: 5% (Massachusetts allows the bank's lower rate). Minnesota: 1%. Illinois Security Deposit Interest Act: 0.005% for leases that started in 2026. New York: whatever the account earns, less 1%.

Is security deposit interest simple or compound?

Simple in San Francisco ("the interest may not be compounded"), Maryland ("Interest is not compounded"), and Minnesota ("simple noncompounded interest"). Connecticut defines accrued interest as "compounded annually to the extent applicable", which matters only when past years' interest wasn't paid. Massachusetts, Ohio, and New York pay it every year, so there's nothing left to compound.

Does a tenant get interest after moving out before a year is up?

It depends on the place. San Francisco: no interest if the tenant leaves before one full year. Massachusetts: the law covers deposits held one year or longer. Maryland: the deposit must be held at least 6 months. Ohio and Illinois: six months. Connecticut and Minnesota: interest accrues from the start, with no minimum stay.

Can the landlord pay the interest as a rent credit?

Yes in Connecticut (the landlord chooses cash or a credit toward the next rent), San Francisco, and Illinois. In Massachusetts the tenant may deduct unpaid interest from the next rent once it's 30 days late.
