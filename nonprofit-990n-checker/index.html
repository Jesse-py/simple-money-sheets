990-N Checker: Has Your Nonprofit Missed Its e-Postcard?

Small nonprofits (PTOs, booster clubs, youth leagues, veterans posts, garden clubs) file a free Form 990-N, the "e-Postcard", every year. Miss three years in a row and the IRS revokes the group's tax-exempt status automatically, often without anyone current noticing, because the reminder goes to whatever address the IRS has on file. Look up your group below: it shows the last year the IRS has an e-Postcard for, which years are missing, and the date exemption would be lost if the next one is missed too.

    How the three-year rule works

    Most tax-exempt groups, other than churches and certain church-related organizations, must file something with the IRS every year. A group whose gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less can file the 990-N, a short online notice with no financial statements and no fee. A group may file Form 990 or 990-EZ instead, and some small groups aren't allowed to use the 990-N at all.

    There is no penalty for filing a 990-N late. The risk is the streak: an organization that files no 990, 990-EZ, or 990-N for three consecutive years loses its tax-exempt status automatically under section 6033(j) of the Internal Revenue Code. The revocation takes effect on the original due date of the third missed return, and the IRS then publishes the group on its Automatic Revocation List.

    A revoked group can't receive tax-deductible donations and is dropped from the IRS list of charities donors check (Publication 78). It may also have to file a federal income tax return (Form 1120 or 1041) and pay income tax. Getting the status back means a new application with a user fee, so filing a free e-Postcard on time is far cheaper.

    When the 990-N is due

    The 990-N is due by the 15th day of the 5th month after your tax year ends, and you can't file it until the year is over.

    Tax year ends990-N due
    December 31 (calendar year)May 15
    June 30 (school-year groups, many PTOs)November 15
    July 31December 15
    August 31January 15
    September 30February 15
    March 31August 15

    If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, it moves to the next business day.

    How to read your result

    • Up to date: the IRS has an e-Postcard for your most recent year that's come due. The checker shows when the next one is due.
    • One year missing: nothing is lost yet. File as soon as you can.
    • Two years missing: the next due date is the revocation date. Filing that year's e-Postcard on time, after the year ends, breaks the streak. Don't wait for the deadline.
    • Revoked: the group is on the IRS Automatic Revocation List. Filing an e-Postcard now won't undo it; you have to apply for reinstatement (below).

    The IRS file holds only each group's most recent e-Postcard, so the checker counts missing years from that one. If your group files Form 990 or 990-EZ instead, or switched to one, the e-Postcard data won't show it, and the checker may report years as missing that you actually filed. The IRS updates both files monthly, so a filing from the last few weeks may not show yet. Confirm anything important in the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search.

    How to file a 990-N

    1. Have ready: the EIN, the group's legal name, any other names it uses, its mailing address, a website address if it has one, the name and address of a principal officer, and the tax year.
    2. On a computer (the IRS says not to use a phone or tablet), go to the IRS Form 990-N page and open the Form 990-N Electronic Filing system. You sign in with an IRS username or an ID.me account (or create one).
    3. Answer the two questions (are gross receipts still normally $50,000 or less, and has the group ended), then submit and save the confirmation.
    4. If the group's mailing address has changed, just enter the new one on the e-Postcard. That address is where the IRS sends its reminders.

    Form 990-N is filed free at IRS.gov, and there is no paper form.

    If your group was revoked

    The IRS reinstates exemption only on a new application: Form 1023, Form 1023-EZ, Form 1024, or Form 1024-A, with the user fee. Revenue Procedure 2014-11 sets out four routes: streamlined retroactive reinstatement, retroactive reinstatement within 15 months of the revocation letter, retroactive reinstatement after 15 months, and reinstatement from the postmark date. The streamlined route is for groups that were eligible to file a 990-EZ or 990-N for the three missed years, haven't been revoked before, and apply within 15 months of the later of the revocation letter (CP-120A) or the date they appeared on the revocation list. See the IRS page How to have your tax-exempt status reinstated, and consider asking a tax professional which route fits.
