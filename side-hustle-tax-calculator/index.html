Side Hustle Tax Calculator

Have a regular job and money coming in on the side (freelancing, gig apps, reselling, a small business)? Your employer only withholds tax on your paycheck, so the side income can leave you owing at tax time. Enter both to see how much federal tax the side income adds, what share of it to set aside, and how much extra to have withheld from each paycheck so you don't need quarterly payments at all. Why side income can leave you owing

Your employer figures withholding from your Form W-4 and your salary alone. It doesn't know about your side income, so nothing is set aside for the two taxes that income brings:

  • Income tax at your top bracket. Side profit stacks on top of your salary, so it's taxed at your highest rate (12%, 22%, 24% and up), less the 20% qualified business income deduction most small side businesses get.
  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% of 92.35% of your profit (Social Security and Medicare, both halves), once that amount reaches $400. W-2 wages use up the Social Security wage base first ($184,500 in 2026, $192,300 in 2027), so a high salary leaves only the 2.9% Medicare part. The self-employment tax calculator breaks this out line by line.

The IRS expects tax to be paid during the year. You avoid an underpayment penalty if what you prepay (withholding plus estimated payments) covers at least 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's (110% if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000), or if you'd owe less than $1,000 at filing.

Extra withholding vs quarterly payments

You can cover the side income tax either way, and you can mix them:

  • Extra withholding. Give your employer a new Form W-4 and enter an extra amount per paycheck in Step 4(c). Form W-4's own instructions say that if you want to pay income and self-employment tax on self-employment income through withholding, you can; the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator is its suggested way to figure the amount, and this calculator does the same arithmetic in one screen.
  • Quarterly estimated payments with Form 1040-ES (or IRS Direct Pay), due in April, June, September, and January. The quarterly estimated tax calculator gives the amount and dates.

Withholding has one big advantage: for the penalty, the IRS treats tax withheld from wages as paid in four equal parts on the four due dates, no matter when in the year it was actually taken out (Form 2210 instructions). So if you realize in the fall that you're behind, a bigger withholding amount for the rest of the year still counts toward the earlier quarters. A late estimated payment doesn't: the penalty for a missed quarter runs from its due date. Withholding also happens on its own, so there's nothing to remember.

Example

A single filer earns a $60,000 salary in 2026, paid every two weeks, with $193 of federal income tax withheld from each check ($5,018 for the year, close to the $5,020 of tax on the salary alone). They also make $15,000 of profit from weekend photography.

  • The side income adds $3,923 of federal tax: $2,119 of self-employment tax plus $1,803 more income tax. That's 26.2% of the side profit.
  • Total tax for the year is $8,943, so 90% of it is $8,049. Withholding covers $5,018, which leaves $3,031 to prepay to avoid a penalty.
  • Option 1: add $117 per paycheck in Step 4(c) of a new W-4 for all 26 paychecks ($151 per paycheck to owe nothing at filing).
  • Option 2: four quarterly estimated payments of $758, then about $894 more when filing.

What this calculator doesn't cover

  • State and local tax. Most states tax side income too, and their withholding is set on a separate state form.
  • Credits and itemized deductions, such as the child tax credit, which lower your total tax. Use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator if you have them, or the quarterly estimated tax calculator, which takes credits and other income.
  • Two jobs, or a working spouse with their own withholding: enter your spouse's wages here, but add their withholding per paycheck into yours only if it's paid on the same schedule. Otherwise the estimator is more accurate.
  • Uneven income. If most of your side income comes late in the year, the annualized income method on Form 2210 can lower or remove an early-quarter penalty.
  • Hobby income (no profit motive) isn't subject to self-employment tax, and hobby expenses aren't deductible.

FAQ

Do I have to pay tax on a small side hustle?

Yes, side income is taxable income no matter how small, and you report it even if you don't get a Form 1099. Self-employment tax only starts once 92.35% of your profit is $400 or more (about $433 of profit), and you won't owe a penalty for underpaying if you'd owe less than $1,000 at filing.

How much should I set aside from side income?

For federal tax, about 23% to 32% of your profit (income minus expenses) for a single filer with a $40,000 to $120,000 salary, and less if your salary is lower; see the table above or enter your own numbers. Add your state's rate on top.

Can I just have more taken out of my paycheck instead of paying quarterly?

Yes. Enter an extra amount per pay period in Step 4(c) of a new Form W-4 and give it to your employer. Withholding pays both income tax and self-employment tax on your Form 1040, and it counts as paid evenly through the year.

I'm already partway through the year. Is it too late?

No. Set "Paychecks left this year" to what's left, and the calculator spreads the amount over those checks. Because withheld tax is treated as paid evenly across the year, catching up through withholding late in the year can still avoid a penalty for earlier quarters.

What if my side business loses money?

A net loss means no self-employment tax, and a loss from a real business (not a hobby) generally lowers your other taxable income, which can mean a refund of some withholding. Enter a negative profit to see it.

Does this calculator store my data?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
