Home Office Deduction Calculator

If you're self-employed and use part of your home only for your business, you can deduct it on Schedule C in one of two ways: the simplified method ($5 per square foot, up to 300 square feet) or the regular method (your actual home costs times the share of your home the office takes up). This calculator works out both, tells you which is bigger, and estimates how much 2026 federal tax it saves. Everything runs in your browser.

Enter yearly amounts, or only the months you used the office if you started or stopped partway through 2026. "Home's value excluding land" is the smaller of what you paid plus improvements or what the house was worth when you started using the office, without the land (your property tax bill usually splits land and building). Profit is Schedule C line 29: business income minus your other business expenses. The tax estimate assumes the standard deduction.

You pick a method each year on your return, and you can switch from one year to the next.

Quick rule: which method is bigger?

With the regular method, your deduction is the office's share of your whole home's yearly costs. With the simplified method, it's $5 for each square foot of office. So, for an office of up to 300 square feet, the regular method wins whenever your home costs more than $5 per square foot per year to run, counting rent or mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, utilities, repairs, and depreciation. For a 1,600 square foot home, that's $8,000 a year.

Most renters clear that easily, because rent alone is usually several times $5 per square foot. Homeowners with a small or paid-off mortgage may not, and for them the simplified method can be close or better, with far less paperwork.

Who can claim it

  • Exclusive and regular use. The space has to be used only for your business, and regularly. A guest room you also work in, or the kitchen table, doesn't count. Storage of inventory or samples and licensed daycare are the exceptions (see Pub 587).
  • Principal place of business, or where you meet clients. Your home qualifies if you do your administrative and management work there (billing, bookkeeping, ordering) and have no other fixed location where you do much of it, even if your actual work happens elsewhere. A separate structure like a studio or detached garage used only for the business also qualifies.
  • Self-employed, not employees. The Form 8829 instructions say employees can't deduct expenses for business use of their home on their personal return, even if they work from home every day. This calculator is for Schedule C filers.
  • Limited to your profit. The deduction can't be more than your business profit before it (gross income from the business minus your other business expenses). With the regular method, expenses over the limit carry forward to next year, depreciation last.

How the calculator works

Simplified: $5 times the office area, capped at 300 square feet, times the months you used it divided by 12 (Pub 587 says to average the allowable square footage by month, and a month counts only if you used the office at least 15 days in it), then capped at your profit.

Regular: business percentage = office area ÷ home area. Whole-home costs are multiplied by that percentage; office-only costs count in full. Depreciation is the business share of the building's value times 2.564% for a full year (Pub 946, years 2 to 39) or the first-year percentage from Pub 587 Table 2 for the month you started (2.461% for January down to 0.107% for December). Then the Form 8829 limit applies, with depreciation allowed last. If you take the standard deduction, mortgage interest and property tax are counted with the other expenses, as the Form 8829 instructions direct. If you itemize, the regular method moves their business share off Schedule A, so the income tax saved is smaller than shown here; the self-employment tax saved is the same.

FAQ

What is the simplified home office deduction?

$5 per square foot of the area you use for business, up to 300 square feet, so at most $1,500 for a full year. The rate hasn't changed since the IRS created the method in 2013 (Rev. Proc. 2013-13).

Can I take the home office deduction as a W-2 employee working from home?

No. The Form 8829 instructions say expenses for business use of your home as an employee can't be deducted on your personal return. If you also have self-employment income and a space used only for that business, you can deduct it against that business.

Does the home office deduction lower self-employment tax?

Yes. It goes on Schedule C line 30 and reduces your net profit, which is what self-employment tax is figured on. While your profit is under the Social Security wage base, each $1,000 of deduction saves about $141 of self-employment tax, plus income tax at your bracket rate.

Can I use the simplified method one year and the regular method the next?

Yes, you choose each year. If you go back to the regular method after a simplified year, depreciation is figured with the optional MACRS tables in Pub 946, and this calculator's depreciation won't match exactly.

What happens when I sell my home?

For an office inside your home, you don't have to split the sale between home and business, but you can't exclude the part of your gain equal to depreciation allowed or allowable after May 6, 1997. Years you use the simplified method have no depreciation, so there's nothing to recapture for them.
